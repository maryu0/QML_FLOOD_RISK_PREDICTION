Research Challenge

Challenge 1: Barren Plateau Problem
Gradients vanish exponentially with circuit depth, making training intractable for deep quantum circuits
Challenge 2: Scale Mismatch
Classical gradients (~10-3) dominate quantum gradients (~10-5), causing optimizer to ignore quantum parameters
Challenge 3: Feature Quality
Random classical features early in training provide poor quantum encoding, leading to suboptimal learning

Research Question: How can we effectively train hybrid quantum-classical models for time-series prediction?

Two-Phase Training Methodology

Phase 1: Classical Pre-training
  • Train: LSTM + Dense layers only
  • Frozen: Quantum parameters
  • Optimizer: Adam (lr=0.001)
  • Early stopping: patience=5
  • LR reduction: on plateau
  • Batch size: 32
  • Epochs: 25

Goal: Extract robust temporal features before quantum optimization

Phase 2: Quantum Fine-tuning
  • Frozen: Classical weights
  • Train: Quantum params + output layer
  • Optimizer: Adjoint differentiation
  • LR schedule: Cosine decay (0.03 → 0)
  • Initialization: Uniform [-π/2, π/2]
  • Batch size: 64
  • Epochs: 40

Goal: Optimize quantum parameters with stable classical features

θ*classical → θ*quantum (Sequential Optimization)

Novel Training Methodology

Why Two-Phase Training Works

1
Stable Classical Features
Quantum circuit receives consistent, meaningful input encodings rather than random features from untrained classical layers
2
Gradient Scale Separation
Dedicated quantum optimization with appropriate learning rates prevents optimizer from ignoring small quantum gradients (~10-5)
3
Symmetry Breaking
Non-zero initialization of quantum output weights breaks symmetry that traps gradients at zero during training

Result: Avoids barren plateaus + enables effective quantum parameter training

Discussion & Limitations

Strengths
  • Two-phase training improves R² by 5.69%
  • Addresses barren plateau problem effectively
  • Scalable architecture (shallow 4-layer VQC)
  • Compatible with NISQ devices
  • Efficient gradient computation (adjoint diff.)
Limitations
  • Quantum simulation overhead (slower training)
  • Marginal improvement over LSTM (+0.08%)
  • Dataset may not fully exploit quantum advantages
  • Real quantum hardware evaluation needed
  • 4-qubit implementation (simulation only)
Future Work
  • Execution on real quantum hardware (IBM Quantum, Rigetti)
  • Larger-scale environmental prediction problems
  • Extension to more qubits and deeper circuits
