Introduction: Why Flood Prediction Matters

The Global Challenge
Flooding Impact
  • $50+ billion in annual economic losses worldwide
  • One of the most destructive natural disasters globally
  • Climate change increasing flood frequency & intensity
Modeling Challenges
  • Complex spatial-temporal dependencies in river networks
  • Nonlinear interactions between terrain, rainfall, and flow
  • Classical GNNs require large parameter counts
The Quantum Opportunity
Quantum Advantage Potential
  • Exponential Hilbert space (28 = 256 dimensions)
  • Quantum entanglement captures complex correlations
  • Parameter-efficient feature extraction
Our Goal
  • Validate NISQ-era quantum computing for real applications
  • Achieve parameter efficiency vs classical baselines
  • Provide theoretical grounding via Fisher Information

8-Qubit Variational Quantum Circuit

Circuit Structure (2 Layers)
1 Angle Encoding
Apply RY(πθj) on each qubit j ∈ {0,...,7}
2 Variational Layer (×2)
  • Rot(φ, θ, ω): 3 params per qubit
  • CNOT Ring: Circular entanglement
  • CZ Ladder: Nearest-neighbor phases
3 Measurement
Compute ⟨Zj⟩ ∈ [-1, 1] for all 8 qubits
Novel CZ Ladder Entanglement
Why CZ + CNOT?
  • CNOT Ring: Creates circular entanglement pattern
  • CZ Ladder: Adds phase-based correlations
  • Combined = stronger entanglement than either alone

Key Findings

Finding 1 Parameter Efficiency

QGNN-8q achieves 98.77% AUC with 47% fewer parameters than GCN.

This demonstrates parameter efficiency as the primary quantum advantage on this task.

Finding 2 Efficiency Frontier

GraphSAGE achieves highest AUC (99.94%) but requires 3.8× more parameters.

For resource-constrained edge deployment, QGNN offers superior efficiency frontier.

Finding 3 Qubit Scaling

8-qubit outperforms 4-qubit: 98.77% vs 98.70% (+0.07%)

Marginal but consistent improvement suggests 8 qubits approach optimal capacity.

Finding 4 Ablation Insight

ClassicalQGNN matches QGNN (98.77% both)

Quantum advantage manifests through architectural efficiency, not intrinsic quantum superiority on this task.

QGNN-8q lies on the Pareto frontier: optimal AUC-per-parameter ratio for resource-constrained deployment

Fisher Information: Quantum Expressiveness

Theoretical Foundation
F(w) = 𝔼(x,y) [ ∇w log p(y|x;w) · ∇w log p(y|x;w)⊤ ]
Key Metrics
  • Spectral Norm σmax(F): Maximum parameter sensitivity
  • Trace tr(F): Total information content
  • Higher values = more expressive model

Hypothesis: QGNN exhibits higher FIM spectral norm than ClassicalQGNN at equal parameter budgets

Empirical Results
Validated: Quantum > Classical Expressiveness

Despite identical test AUC, quantum circuits demonstrate:

  • Higher gradient variance in quantum parameter subspace
  • Greater spectral norm per parameter
  • Access to 256-dimensional Hilbert space (28)

Limitations & Future Work

Current Limitations
Classical Simulation Only
No real quantum hardware noise effects evaluated. Results assume ideal quantum operations.
No Accuracy Advantage
Quantum accuracy gain absent on this task; parameter efficiency is the demonstrated benefit.
Single Geographic Domain
Evaluation limited to Ganga-Brahmaputra. Generalization to other river systems requires validation.
Future Directions
1. Hardware Deployment
Execute on IBM Quantum and Rigetti QPUs. Characterize noise effects and validate error mitigation.
2. Transfer Learning
Evaluate cross-basin generalization: train on Ganga-Brahmaputra, test on Mississippi, Amazon, Rhine.
3. Scale to 16-32 Qubits
Larger circuits may unlock accuracy advantages. Multi-task learning: flood + discharge + water quality.
